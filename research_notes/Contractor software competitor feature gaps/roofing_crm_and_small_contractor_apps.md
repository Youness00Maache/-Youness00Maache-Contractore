# Roofing/exterior CRMs, photo documentation, and lightweight estimating/invoicing apps (2025–2026)

Scope: (a) JobNimbus, AccuLynx, Roofr, CompanyCam, plus Hover as a clear 2026 top player for photo-based measurement; (b) Joist, Invoice2go, Knowify, QuickBooks Online (estimates/progress invoicing/AI reminders). Jobber is excluded (covered elsewhere).

Source-quality notes for the report writer:
- Many "2026 review" pages are SEO/affiliate sites, and several are published by competitors: Workyard (competes with Joist, and discloses it), ScanManifold, FieldCamp, and QuoteIQ (compete with CompanyCam/Roofr). Their numbers are useful but biased. I fetched the vendor pricing pages directly where possible: CompanyCam, Joist, Roofr, and Invoice2go (no prices shown on its page). JobNimbus and AccuLynx publish no prices.
- No Reddit threads came up in three Reddit-oriented searches. Customer-voice evidence comes from App Store/Google Play/Capterra/G2/Trustpilot, either quoted through review aggregators or coded by Workyard (1,400 Joist reviews).
- Today's date is 2026-11-13. Anything from before 2025 is flagged.

## Q1. Which features are most praised and most used?

### Takeaway
Across both groups, the features customers praise most are (1) speed in the field: quotes and invoices done on site, photos that file themselves to the job, and an AI walkthrough that turns voice and photos into a report; (2) professional-looking client output: branded estimates, photo reports, and good/better/best proposals with e-signature; and (3) getting paid: deposits, card/ACH links, payment schedules, financing, and automated reminders. Roofing-specific tools add instant estimates and aerial measurements that win leads. Vendors report big lead-conversion numbers from these, but the figures are self-reported.

### Cited Findings

**CompanyCam (photo documentation)**
- Ratings are high and the review base is large: App Store 4.8 (about 30,000 ratings), Google Play 4.7 (about 7,880), Capterra 4.7 (103), G2 4.7 (32). The company's Google Business Profile is 3.7 (142), with support and refund complaints. — [FieldCompare, Sept 2026](https://fieldcompare.com/companycam-review)
- Most-praised capability: photos and videos are timestamped and GPS-tagged and "file themselves under the project", giving an auto-organized project history. Capture works offline and syncs later, and photos "live in the cloud rather than the camera roll". — [FieldCompare](https://fieldcompare.com/companycam-review); [CompanyCam pricing page](https://companycam.com/pricing)
- Reviewers credit CompanyCam's "annotation tools" and "client-facing timelines" over competitors, and cite before/after photos as proof for disputed invoices. — [FieldCompare](https://fieldcompare.com/companycam-review)
- A G2 reviewer describes before-and-after galleries, reports, and image annotation. A Capterra reviewer praised "absolute proof of issues that were on the project before we got there". (Quotes are from search-result excerpts of the review pages.) — [G2](https://www.g2.com/sellers/companycam); [Capterra reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/171143/CompanyCam/reviews/)
- AI Walkthrough: the user records a walkthrough and takes photos while narrating, and Walkthrough Note turns the voice-to-text and photos into an editable document. A Capterra reviewer called it the best feature they had "yet to see in a project management application". — [CompanyCam blog](https://companycam.com/resources/blog/best-ai-tools-for-contractors); [Capterra reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/171143/CompanyCam/reviews/)

- Proposals: good/better/best templates with material photos, warranty language, and financing options, plus e-signature. The reviewer says users credit them with higher close rates. — [RSG Roofr review](https://roofingsoftwareguide.com/reviews/roofr-review/)

- Instant Estimator: a website widget that gives homeowners a price range from their address and drops the lead and address into Roofr. Vendor case studies:
  - Maven Roofing: the Instant Estimator "closes 25% of all leads", and a $52k job closed within 7 days of adding it with no paid ads. — [Roofr case study](https://roofr.com/case-studies/amanda-veinott)
  - Maven Roofing reported a 42.8% win rate on Instant Estimator leads in an April 2025 masterclass. — [Roofr speed-to-lead page](https://roofr.com/more-info/speed-to-lead-roofing-software); [masterclass](https://roofr.com/masterclass/instant-estimator-and-lead-tracking-with-amanda-veinott)

  - All of these are vendor-published, mix different metrics, and have no independent verification. In the same masterclass, the Maven owner called the lift a "small amount of differential". — [Roofr masterclass](https://roofr.com/masterclass/instant-estimator-and-lead-tracking-with-amanda-veinott)
- Payments: deposits and progress payments attach to the job via Roofr Payments (card and ACH). GoodLeap homeowner financing is available on Essentials and Scale. — [RSG](https://roofingsoftwareguide.com/reviews/roofr-review/); [Roofr pricing](https://roofr.com/pricing)
- Job boards (kanban pipeline): Starter gets 1 basic board, Scale gets 7 customizable boards. Material ordering is listed even on the free Starter plan. — [Roofr pricing](https://roofr.com/pricing)

**Knowify (small commercial/specialty subs)**
- The core value is billing by contract type. Invoices auto-configure for time & materials, fixed price, or AIA pay applications (G702/G703), using automated schedules of values, retainage tracking, and change-order reconciliation. Invoice and pay-app status syncs with QuickBooks Online. — [Knowify AIA contracts & invoicing](https://knowify.com/resources/aia-contracts-and-invoicing/); [Knowify construction invoice guide](https://www.knowify.com/blog/construction-invoice/)

**QuickBooks Online (what contractors use for estimates/invoices)**
- Progress invoicing works like this: create one estimate for the whole job, then bill by percentage, a dollar amount, or specific lines, and the remaining balance updates automatically. A report ties estimates to their progress invoices. Sources disagree on tiers (Plus/Advanced, versus "Essentials has no native progress billing or retainage tracking"). — [QuickBooks help (CA)](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-ca/help-article/invoicing/set-send-progress-invoices-quickbooks-online/L0Ymm6WjR_CA_en_CA); [ConstructionCoverage 2026](https://constructioncoverage.com/software/accounting/quickbooks-for-contractors)

- Automated payment reminders are the most-evidenced "get paid" feature. Intuit says AI-drafted reminders get businesses paid on average about 5 days faster. The figure comes from Intuit's own beta comparison (Jan–Aug 2024, so older data). — [Intuit blog](https://www.intuit.com/blog/innovative-thinking/delivering-done-for-you); [Accounting Today](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/intuit-debuts-ai-agents-for-quickbooks)
- Intuit's 2026 late-payments report: 59% of small businesses have invoices overdue by 30+ days, up from 47% the year before. (Intuit-reported, via search summary.) — [Newsfile, Jul 2025 release and coverage](https://www.newsfilecorp.com/release/260641/QuickBooks-New-AI-Agents-Explained-What-Each-One-Does-Expert-Consumers-Report)

### Inferences
- For a cheap, mobile-first app, the highest-value praised features are the ones that cost little to build and are not roofing-specific:
  - photo timelines per job with annotation and before/after pairs;
  - a voice/photo walkthrough that becomes a report (ContractorDocs' AI already drafts from dictation, so adding photos is incremental);
  - deposits and payment schedules with a real pay button;
  - automated overdue reminders;
  - good/better/best options on estimates;
  - review requests after a job is paid.
- Aerial measurements, supplier ordering/price sync, and an instant-estimate website widget are clearly valued by roofers, but they depend on per-report imagery costs or supplier partnerships. They are poor fits at a $5–$10 price point. At most, offer integrations or links to them.
- The roofing CRMs converge on the same pipeline: job board, automations, proposal with options, e-sign, payments plus financing, and supplier ordering. The solo apps converge on: fast quote on site, deposits, a pay link, reminders, and reviews.

### Gaps
- No Reddit threads surfaced (r/Roofing, r/Contractor, r/handyman), so first-person community sentiment is missing.
- No usage telemetry (which features are *used* most) is public for any vendor. "Most used" can only be inferred from review-mention counts (Workyard/Joist) and vendor marketing.
- Some AccuLynx and JobNimbus feature claims come from third-party roundups. Official pages for supplier price sync were not fetched.

## Q2. What do small users complain about, and what makes them switch?

### Takeaway
Price is the main complaint everywhere: per-seat fees, price hikes, features moving to higher tiers, and renewal shock. The next most common are data held hostage after cancelling, crashes or lost work, unreachable support, and payment fees or slow payouts. Small roofers find the full roofing CRMs too expensive per seat and too heavy. Solo app users outgrow them when they need team logins, crew time tracking, or accounting sync.

### Cited Findings
- **Joist** (Workyard's coding of 1,400 reviews; Workyard is a competitor):
  - "price increases and features moved to higher plans": 279 mentions, the most common complaint
  - records locked without a subscription: 88. One reviewer wrote: "Once your subscription ends, you no longer have access to any of your data."
  - freezes, crashes, and lost work: 75
  - support hard to reach: 70
  - payout and fee issues: 52
  Google Play logged 51 one-star reviews in August 2024, when the free plan ended (older). — [Workyard](https://www.workyard.com/compare/joist-review)
- **Joist switch triggers** seen in Workyard's sales calls:
  - needing crew time tracking, GPS, or payroll (7 small contractors were looking at a separate tool for crew hours);
  - copying hours into invoices by hand;
  - single-login limits for office staff or foremen;
  - price hikes.
  — [Workyard](https://www.workyard.com/compare/joist-review)
- **Joist reviewers** say there is "no phone number to call" for support, call the processing fees "scamming", and complain that Run costs about $1,000 a year. — [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details/Joist+App+for+Contractors?id=com.joistapp.android.joist&hl=en_CA); [Struvia](https://struvia.co/blog/joist-app-pricing)
- **Invoice2go**:
  - Price hikes are the main complaint. A Trustpilot reviewer's annual plan went from $35.99 to $53.98 (about +50%), and another left for Xero after £299 became £450. — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/invoice.2go.com)
  - Users object to being upgraded to a pricier plan without asking. — [Business.org](https://www.business.org/finance/payment-processing/invoice2go-review/)
  - Reviewers say "the best features are now locked behind the more expensive plans", and describe renewal price jumps. — [Software Advice reviews](https://www.softwareadvice.com/accounting/invoice2go-profile/reviews/)
  - Counterpoint: 81% of 41 Capterra raters still rate its value good or excellent. — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/81858/Invoice2go/pricing/)
- **CompanyCam**:
  - Capterra reviewers name price as the top complaint. Extra users cost $29–$34 each, so a 10-tech shop on Crew pays about $322/month "for photos alone". — [FieldCompare](https://fieldcompare.com/companycam-review)
  - It has no scheduling or dispatch, so teams need a second tool. — [FieldCompare](https://fieldcompare.com/companycam-review); [RSG CompanyCam review](https://roofingsoftwareguide.com/reviews/companycam-review/)
  - AI credits are metered: 10 on Core is "a taster". — [FieldCompare](https://fieldcompare.com/companycam-review)
  - Proposals are capped at 5/month on Core and 15/month on Crew. — [RSG CompanyCam review](https://roofingsoftwareguide.com/reviews/companycam-review/)
- **Roofr** (review publisher's analysis, not direct quotes):
  - Starter's 10 trial documents, 5 automations, and lack of payments push users to $249/month Essentials.
  - Per-report fees inflate busy months.
  - QuickBooks is Scale-only and in beta.
  - CRM automation is shallow (no lead scoring or drip branching).
  - A 2-person shop that only needs measurements may overpay.
  - The same source says there is "no native app or offline mode". I could not verify this, and it may be outdated or wrong.
  — [RSG Roofr review](https://roofingsoftwareguide.com/reviews/roofr-review/)
- **AccuLynx**:
  - Its per-user cost "penalizes small teams". — [RSG AccuLynx vs JobNimbus](https://roofingsoftwareguide.com/comparisons/acculynx-vs-jobnimbus/)
  - Hidden add-on fees for SmartDocs (contracts and e-signature), texting, and integrations. — [Fervor Studio](https://fervorstudio.ca/news/acculynx-review-pricing-alternatives/)
  - AI features "require significant data input", which delays ROI. — [BuilderLync](https://builderlync.com/blog/acculynx-review-pricing-features-2026)
  - Capterra includes a reviewer whose company switched away from AccuLynx. — [Capterra AccuLynx reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/116187/Acculynx/reviews/?page=3)
- **JobNimbus**: one Capterra reviewer found it "more confusing and a lot more work to set up". Texting (Engage) and AI are paid extras on top of base and per-user fees. — [RSG AccuLynx vs JobNimbus (citing Capterra)](https://roofingsoftwareguide.com/comparisons/acculynx-vs-jobnimbus/); [Contractor ToolStack](https://contractortoolstack.com/guides/jobnimbus-ai-features-guide/)
- **Knowify**: one review says job costing is limited to the Advanced tier ("The $99 Plan Skips Job Costing"), and another says the QuickBooks sync is one-way. — [Fervor Studio Knowify](https://fervorstudio.ca/news/knowify-review-pricing-alternatives/); [search summary of Knowify reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/135791/Knowify/)
- **QuickBooks**: per 2026 guides, Essentials-level plans lack native progress billing and retainage, so contractors either upgrade or add a tool. — [ConstructionCoverage](https://constructioncoverage.com/software/accounting/quickbooks-for-contractors)

### Inferences
- ContractorDocs' $0/$5/$10 pricing, combined with a "your data stays accessible/exportable on Free" promise, directly answers the top two complaints about Joist and Invoice2go: price hikes and data locked after cancelling. This is worth stating explicitly in marketing.
- Team logins without per-seat fees, and turning time sheets into invoices, are the concrete switch triggers away from solo apps. ContractorDocs already has time sheet and material log documents. Linking them into a T&M invoice would capture this switch.
- Reliability ("lost work") is a top-3 complaint. Saving offline drafts locally and syncing them later matters more than new features.

### Gaps
- No direct Reddit quotes. Sentiment on CompanyCam's 2026 tier restructure comes from competitor blogs only ([ScanManifold](https://www.scanmanifold.com/blog-posts/companycam-pricing-2026-complete-guide-f0fd7)). I found no first-party customer reaction.
- JobNimbus and AccuLynx small-user complaints are thin. Most evidence is aggregated by review publishers.

## Q3. Current pricing (2025–2026) and what is gated

### Takeaway
The roofing CRMs cost $109–$349+/month (Roofr) or are quote-only at roughly $225–$300+/month plus seats (JobNimbus, AccuLynx). CompanyCam is $63–$299/month with seat fees. The solo apps cost $6–$40/month (Invoice2go, Joist), but gate unlimited documents, recurring invoices, change orders, reviews, reports, and accounting sync behind upper tiers. Payments, financing, AI receptionists, SMS, and measurement reports are usually metered add-ons. ContractorDocs at $5/$10 sits below every competitor's useful tier.

### Cited Findings

  - Crew $129/month: 3 users.
  - Scale $199/month: 3 users.
  - Enterprise: custom, 50+ users.
  - Extra users $29. Annual saves 20%.
  - Marketing Suite add-on $99/$79 (review requests, Google Business Profile sync, social content, website gallery).

  — [CompanyCam pricing](https://companycam.com/pricing)

## Q4. AI features shipped in 2025–2026 and their reception

### Takeaway
2025–2026 AI in this segment falls into five groups: (1) AI phone receptionists, the most common and the most monetized (JobNimbus AssistAI, Joist, Roofr); (2) voice/photo-to-document tools (CompanyCam AI Walkthrough, Joist "Estimate with AI"); (3) a voice-controlled CRM agent (JobNimbus Scout, still in beta); (4) AI payment reminders and agents (QuickBooks); and (5) AI-built websites, lead scoring, and photo-to-3D (Roofr Sites, AccuLynx, Hover). Reception is positive for walkthrough reports and receptionists, according to vendor-reported metrics. Draft quality and the metered limits draw criticism.

### Inferences
Highest-value, cheapest-to-build gaps for a $0/$5/$10 mobile-first app, ordered by the strength of evidence:
1. **Automated overdue-payment reminders.** Invoice2go includes them on every tier, and QBO claims payment about 5 days faster. ContractorDocs already sends from Gmail and has AI email drafting.
2. **Native pay button with paid-status sync** (Stripe Connect or PayPal checkout webhooks). Payments are on every competitor's core tier.
3. **Per-job photo timeline with annotation and a before/after photo report**, plus an AI walkthrough that turns photos and voice into a report. This is CompanyCam's most-praised feature set, and it costs $63–$79/month there.
4. **Good/better/best options on estimates**, with the client picking an option in the portal before e-signing. Roofr and AccuLynx shipped this in 2026.
5. **Review request after payment.** Several competitors gate it to $32+ plans or a $79–$99 add-on.
6. **Offline drafting with sync later.** CompanyCam's offline capture is praised, and "lost work" is a top-3 Joist complaint.
7. **A simple lead/job board view** over existing jobs.
8. **Turning time sheets and material logs into a T&M invoice.** This addresses Joist's switch trigger.

Lower priority or partner-only: QuickBooks sync, financing referral links, aerial measurements (link out to Roofr/Hover/EagleView reports), supplier ordering, AI receptionist, and AIA G702/G703 (a niche commercial need).

### Gaps
- The repo checks were keyword greps, not a full feature audit. Photo handling in the AI assistant, team seats, and time-sheet-to-invoice flows should be confirmed by reading the code.
- There is no evidence on which of these features small contractors would actually pay $5–$10 for. That would need user interviews or surveys.
