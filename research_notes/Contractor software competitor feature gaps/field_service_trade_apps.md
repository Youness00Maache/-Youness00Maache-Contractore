# Field-service / home-service trade software (ServiceTitan, Jobber, Housecall Pro, Workiz, FieldEdge, simPRO, Tradify, Fergus, ServiceM8): features customers value, complaints, pricing, AI, and gaps vs ContractorDocs

Research date: 2026-11-13. Source-quality note for the report writer: official vendor pricing/feature/help pages and vendor blogs were fetched directly where possible (Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, Tradify, Fergus pricing pages; Housecall Pro help center; ServiceTitan Pantheon blogs; Jobber llm-info page). Review-site data (G2, Capterra, Software Advice) was mostly reached through search-result summaries because those sites and Reddit block automated fetching. Many "2026 review/pricing" pages on `*.pages.dev` domains and competitor blogs (FieldCamp, OneCrew, QuoteIQ, ServiceAgent, US Tech Automations) are affiliate or competitor marketing; they are flagged where used. Reddit could not be searched directly (domain blocked for this tool); Reddit quotes below are second-hand.

## Q1. Which features do reviewers and case studies repeatedly praise as the reason they buy or stay?

### Takeaway
The features that show up again and again as reasons to buy or stay are: ease of use and a mobile app the crew will actually use; a client portal where customers book, approve quotes and pay online; automated reminders and quote/invoice follow-ups; online card/ACH payments (plus consumer financing for bigger tickets); and, for larger shops, dispatch boards, call booking, memberships/service agreements and good-better-best price books. For phone-driven trades, call tracking and AI call answering are the new reasons to buy. Vendors back these with self-reported numbers (booking rate, average ticket), not independent studies.

### Inferences
- For 1–10-person teams, the "reason to buy" features are customer-facing and cut admin time: online booking, quote and invoice reminders and follow-ups, online pay (card, ACH, deposits, tips), a client portal, and a mobile app crews adopt. Dispatch boards, memberships, call centers and fleet tools matter mainly to the ServiceTitan/FieldEdge segment (HVAC and plumbing with many techs).
- Consumer financing is pushed hardest by Housecall Pro and Jobber because it lifts ticket size, which helps vendors that also earn on payments. The evidence comes almost entirely from vendors or financing partners.
- The vendors' upsell ladders (Q3) show what they think customers will pay more for: two-way texting, optional line items (good-better-best), job costing, automations, AI receptionist, marketing suite.

### Gaps
- I could not read Reddit threads directly (blocked for this tool), so all Reddit sentiment here is second-hand from vendor blogs.
- G2 and Capterra pages could not be fetched in full, so pros/cons percentages filtered to 1–10-employee companies were not available. The patterns come from aggregator summaries.
- I found no independent (non-vendor) study measuring the effect of quote follow-ups, online booking or financing on revenue.
- I found little 2025–2026 review evidence on ServiceM8, Tradify and Fergus features specifically; much of the ServiceM8 review base is pre-2019.

## Q2. Which features do customers say are missing, too complex or too expensive, especially solo operators and small teams?

### Takeaway
The complaints are consistent: per-user and tier-jump pricing that climbs fast once a team passes about 3–8 people; core features locked behind expensive tiers (job costing, two-way texting, good-better-best quotes, recurring service plans, QuickBooks sync); unreliable QuickBooks sync; weak reporting; little invoice/template customization; add-on stacking and hidden prices. ServiceTitan, Workiz and simPRO add long contracts, hard cancellations and costly implementation. Solo operators are steered to free or cheap tools (Yardbook, LawnPro, ServiceM8 Free).

### Cited Findings

**Jobber**
- Per-user pricing is the most cited cost complaint: "Once we hit 8 employees, the per-user cost became absurd"; pricing rose "really quickly once we added our fourth tech" (Reddit user, quoted second-hand) — [FieldCamp Jobber review](https://fieldcamp.ai/reviews/jobber)
- QuickBooks sync is the top integration complaint: "QuickBooks sync drops transactions. We've had to manually reconcile every week." Integrations score 6/10 — [FieldCamp Jobber review](https://fieldcamp.ai/reviews/jobber)
- Reporting is the weakest area at 5/10 ("The reports are useless for real business decisions"). Other weak spots: limited invoice-template customization, manual drag-and-drop dispatch, quoting with no change-order support (7.5/10), route optimization still basic (6/10), and no native GPS or geofencing (needs a third-party tool such as Force Fleet Tracking) — [FieldCamp Jobber review](https://fieldcamp.ai/reviews/jobber)
- Going from 1 user to 5 users roughly triples the price (third-party lawn-care site) — [mowbosshq.pages.dev](https://mowbosshq.pages.dev/guides/jobber-alternatives)
- For solo lawn operators, Yardbook (free, with fees only on payments) and LawnPro (Startup about $29/mo annual; free tier) are the usual cheaper picks. One reviewer says Jobber's cost "will pinch a one-truck operation" — [mowbosshq.pages.dev](https://mowbosshq.pages.dev/guides/jobber-alternatives); [crewscalepro.pages.dev](https://crewscalepro.pages.dev/software/jobber-alternatives)

**Housecall Pro**
- Review aggregators summarize users reporting "significant price increases for advanced features or additional users", "steep tier jumps, frequent price increases, and costly add-ons" — [Software Advice Housecall Pro](https://www.softwareadvice.com/field-service/housecall-profile/)
- Capterra attributes 61% of 195 negative reviews to recurring bugs, glitches and slow fixes. Invoice-reminder settings are hard to configure, and invoicing is "cumbersome and inflexible" — [Capterra Housecall Pro](https://www.capterra.com/p/140363/HouseCall-Pro/)
- Permissions are too broad: technicians can see revenue or change appointments, and reviewers ask for role-based permissions — [Capterra Housecall Pro](https://www.capterra.com/p/140363/HouseCall-Pro/)
- Most add-on prices (CSR AI, Pipeline, Payroll, Voice, Campaigns, VGPS, Websites) are not published; the pricing page sends buyers to book a demo — [Housecall Pro pricing](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/)

**ServiceTitan**
- Pricing is not published. Third-party estimates of $250–$500 per technician per month plus implementation fees are not vendor-confirmed. One source cites implementation above $50,000 — [OneCrew ServiceTitan reviews](https://www.getonecrew.com/post/servicetitan-reviews); [Projul analysis](https://projul.com/blog/servicetitan-pricing-analysis-2026/)
- Annual contracts are standard ("if it doesn't work out, you're paying for 12 months regardless"). A G2 reviewer describes no reasonable early exit, and BBB complaints cite cancellation disputes — [OneCrew ServiceTitan reviews](https://www.getonecrew.com/post/servicetitan-reviews)
- Implementation estimates run from 2–4 months to 6–12 months. Onboarding complaints include being onboarded in a group with no one-on-one help — [OneCrew](https://www.getonecrew.com/post/servicetitan-reviews); [SchedulingKit pros/cons](https://schedulingkit.com/pros-and-cons/servicetitan-pros-and-cons)
- Shops with fewer than 5 technicians will likely find it "too expensive and complex" (competitor and affiliate sources) — [ServiceAgent](https://serviceagent.ai/blogs/servicetitan-pricing-explained/); [ToolEdUpPro](https://tooleduppro.pages.dev/reviews/servicetitan)

**Workiz**
- Phone reliability splits reviewers: some report intermittent Genius Phone failures and slow support. One Capterra reviewer dislikes the "copy phone" calling flow — [rfp.wiki Workiz](https://www.rfp.wiki/crm-marketing/field-service-management/workiz); [Capterra Workiz](https://www.capterra.com/p/147525/Workiz/reviews/?page=3)
- Critical reviews cite billing surprises, annual contracts and hard cancellations. Account managers change without notice ("we didn't even know our account manager had changed"). Reporting is "shallower" than competitors' — [rfp.wiki Workiz](https://www.rfp.wiki/crm-marketing/field-service-management/workiz)
- Connecteam's verdict: "solid ... for small and medium-sized businesses, but costs can add up" — [Connecteam Workiz review](https://connecteam.com/reviews/workiz/)

**FieldEdge and simPRO (mid-market)**
- FieldEdge users find advanced features, reports and some setup tasks unintuitive; value-for-money scores (3.9) are the lowest category — [Software Advice FieldEdge](https://www.softwareadvice.com/field-service/fieldedge-profile/)
- A simPRO reviewer pays more than $250 per user licence per month. Another reports NZ$200/user/month plus NZ$50/month for the mobile app, plus paid training for new staff. ERPResearch notes separate office and mobile licences and multi-year contracts — [Capterra IE simPRO](https://www.capterra.ie/reviews/10529/simpro-enterprise); [ERPResearch simPRO](https://erpresearch.com/erp-add-ons/field-service/simpro/pricing)

**ServiceM8 (mostly older reviews)**
- Recurring complaints: Apple-only or Android gaps, costs that rise with job volume, Stripe payment fees, problems printing job-card stickers, a dated desktop view, and an "inventory & parts feature was of little use." Many reviews date from 2016–2018 — [Capterra ServiceM8](https://capterra.com/p/110711/ServiceM8/reviews/); [dupple.com](https://dupple.com/reviews/servicem8)

### Inferences
- Small teams' pain is less about missing features than about features being locked or priced out: Jobber's job costing, two-way texting and optional line items start at Grow ($149–$199/mo for 1 user); Housecall Pro's QuickBooks sync and checklists start at Essentials ($149–$189/mo) and service plans at MAX ($299–$329/mo).
- Unreliable QuickBooks sync, weak or inflexible reporting, and rigid invoice templates are cross-platform weak spots. ContractorDocs' PDF templates, profit analytics and AI-generated reports compete directly there.
- "No change-order support" in Jobber quoting (per FieldCamp) and "inventory of little use" in ServiceM8 point to document-centric gaps that ContractorDocs already covers (change orders, purchase orders, material logs, QR inventory).
- Long contracts and opaque pricing (ServiceTitan, Workiz, simPRO, FieldEdge) push solo and small operators toward transparent, self-serve tools. ContractorDocs' Free/$5/$10 pricing sits well below every paid entry tier found (cheapest paid competitor tiers are about $29/mo).

### Gaps
- I could not filter complaints by company size (1–10 employees) on G2 or Capterra because those pages could not be fetched.
- I did not find direct complaints about missing online booking or SMS among solo users. The evidence that these are "must-haves" comes from vendor tiering and praise, not from complaints about their absence.

## Q3. Current pricing tiers, per-user costs (2025–2026) and what is gated behind higher tiers

### Takeaway
Only Jobber, Housecall Pro, Tradify, Fergus and (via third parties) ServiceM8 publish prices. ServiceTitan, FieldEdge and simPRO are quote-only, and Workiz hid its prices in May 2026. Entry prices for one user run from about $29/mo (Jobber Core annual, ServiceM8 Starter) to $79/mo (Housecall Pro Basic monthly). Prices for a small team (5 users) cluster around $150–$330/mo. Per-technician enterprise tools are estimated at $125–$500 per tech per month. The features gated to higher tiers are the ones listed in Q1.

### Inferences
- The vendors consistently gate automation (follow-ups, reminders, workflows), two-way SMS, accounting sync, job costing, recurring service plans and AI call answering to mid or upper tiers. That is where a $5–$10 product can undercut them on value.
- Tradify and Fergus price per user (about $47–$61 per user for Tradify). Jobber and Housecall Pro use team-size bands; ServiceM8 prices by job volume with unlimited users. ContractorDocs' flat Free/$5/$10 is far below all of them, but they bundle payments, SMS and booking, which earn them processing and messaging revenue.
- ServiceM8's free tier (30 jobs/mo, unlimited AI-lite use) is the closest direct "free" competitor in this set.

### Gaps
- The official ServiceM8 pricing page was not fetched; the figures are from third parties and currencies are inconsistent.
- Fergus's per-user vs flat price is ambiguous on its own page.
- No verified 2026 prices exist for ServiceTitan, FieldEdge, simPRO or current Workiz plans (all quote-based).
- Housecall Pro's add-on prices (CSR AI, Voice, Campaigns, etc.) are not published.

## Q4. What AI features have these platforms shipped in 2025–2026, and how are they received?

### Takeaway
Every major player now sells an AI receptionist / call-and-text answering agent (Jobber Receptionist, Housecall Pro CSR AI, ServiceTitan Virtual Agents, Workiz Genius Answering, ServiceM8 Office Agent). Most also ship free or included "assistant" AI: report building, drafting quotes, descriptions and marketing copy, and business coaching. ServiceTitan is furthest toward autonomous agents (Max with about 30 agents; Atlas). Reception is cautiously positive but evidence is thin: vendors cite booking-rate gains, independent reviewers call the receptionists "promising but new", and some note robotic voices and high-tier gating.

### Cited Findings

**Jobber**
- **Receptionist** (AI call and text answering) became generally available in mid-August 2025. It was included with Plus and available as an add-on for Grow, as part of the "Jobber AI" platform — [PM Engineer](https://www.pmmag.com/articles/106754-jobber-launches-ai-powered-receptionist); [Green Industry Pros](https://www.greenindustrypros.com/business-management/software/product/22951270/jobber-jobber-launches-aipowered-receptionist)
- The Nov 2026 pricing page lists AI Receptionist as a $29/mo add-on and a Plus feature — [Jobber pricing](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/)
- Receptionist answers calls and texts after hours, books jobs within business-defined rules, takes messages and transfers calls on keywords — [Jobber llm-info](https://www.getjobber.com/llm-info/)
- It was later extended to "do more to assist your existing clients" — [Jobber product updates](https://productupdates.getjobber.com/121638-receptionist-now-does-more-to-assist-your-existing-clients)
- Reception: FieldCamp scores Receptionist 6.5/10, "promising but new" (competitor source) — [FieldCamp](https://fieldcamp.ai/reviews/jobber)
- Stork.ai notes some older clients may perceive the voice as robotic and that it required Plus rather than a cheap standalone option — [Stork.ai](https://www.stork.ai/en/jobber-ai-receptionist)

**Prioritization inference for a $5/$10 small-contractor product**
1. The highest-leverage gap is online payments (card/ACH) and deposits in the client portal, with automated payment and quote reminders. These appear at Jobber Core/Connect and every Housecall Pro tier and are praised as "how we get paid".
2. Next are SMS notifications and two-way texting, plus online booking and request forms. They are cheap to bolt onto the existing portal and calendar.
3. Then recurring jobs and maintenance agreements, good-better-best option quotes, and review requests. All are locked to $149–$329/mo tiers at competitors, so they make a strong value pitch at $10.
4. Then QuickBooks/Xero export or sync (reliability is the competitors' top complaint) and live clock-in/out with optional location stamp.
5. Defer or partner for dispatch boards, route optimization, fleet GPS, AI phone receptionist and marketing suites. These serve larger shops and carry ongoing telephony costs.

- I did not research other 2026 contenders (FieldPulse, Service Fusion, GorillaDesk, Joist, Kickserv, Zuper, Commusoft) because of the tool-call budget. They should be checked if the report needs a wider market view.
