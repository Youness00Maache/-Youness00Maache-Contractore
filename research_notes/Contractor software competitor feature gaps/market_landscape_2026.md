# Contractor Software Market Landscape 2025–2026 (construction management, trades FSM, estimating/invoicing apps)

Research date: 2026-11-13. Figures are 2025–2026 unless flagged **[OLDER]**. Several pricing and funding numbers come from third-party aggregators and conflict with each other. Those are flagged **[AGGREGATOR]**. Where possible I checked the vendor's own pricing page or SEC filing.

## 1. Which vendors lead (customers, revenue, funding, M&A) and why

### Takeaway
The market splits into two public anchors and a group of PE-backed mid-market leaders. Procore (US$1.32B revenue, 17,850 customers) is the anchor for commercial and GC construction management. ServiceTitan (US$961M revenue, ~10,800 customers, $82B gross transaction volume) is the anchor for larger residential trades shops. Jobber (100,000+ customers) and Housecall Pro (200K+ "pros") lead by headcount in small and mid-size home services, and Buildertrend leads residential builders and remodelers. Money is moving toward payments and AI: ServiceTitan earns from GTV, CompanyCam bought a fintech, Buildertrend launched Bill Pay, and the Verisk/AccuLynx $2.35B deal valued roofing CRM highly.

### Inferences
- The two public leaders monetize through transactions as well as seats. ServiceTitan reports GTV ($82B) next to revenue, and Procore is building Procore Pay. For SMB-focused rivals, payment processing is now a core revenue line, not an add-on.

- The 2025–2026 strategic moves (BizJet AI, Datagrid, Beam, Payapps) point the same way: everyone is buying AI agents and payment/fintech capability.

## 2. What contractors most want from software (survey statistics)

### Takeaway
No neutral 2025–2026 survey ranks individual features for small contractors. The best evidence comes from review-platform importance ratings, vendor-run surveys, and which features vendors put on entry tiers. Across these, the table-stakes set is: invoicing/billing with online payments, scheduling and job management, a usable mobile app, QuickBooks sync, and fast customer communication. Most contractors already "have" FSM software but use it shallowly. AI demand centers on customer communication and estimating.

### Cited Findings
- GetApp contractor-software reviewer importance ratings (updated Mar 2026): 92% rated multiple-project management important or highly important, 91% billing and invoicing, 91% job management/scheduling. These are reviewer ratings, not a formal survey. — [GetApp electronic payments filter page](https://www.getapp.com/operations-management-software/contractor-management/f/electronic-payments) (cited via search snippet)
- ACCA / Farmington Consulting "2025 Contractor of the Future Study" (1,000+ HVACR contractors): 56% have field service management software, but most underutilize it. Full results are members-only. — [ACCA release](https://www.acca.org/news/release/acca-and-farmington-consulting-group-release-2025-contractor-of-the-future-study-results); [PHCP Pros](https://www.phcppros.com/articles/22596-acca-and-farmington-consulting-group-release-2025-contractor-of-the-future-study-results)
- IFS study of trade contractors: 85% have maintenance contracts with customer-specific terms, pricing and service levels, but only 14% say their software handles these contracts "very well". **[date not confirmed; may be older than 2025]** — [Field Service News](https://www.fieldservicenews.com/ifs-study-trade-contractors-struggle-to-realize-aftermarket-service-revenue-potential-due-to-obsolete-software/)
- Sage / AGC 2025 Construction Hiring & Business Outlook (1,100+ contractors): mobile software adoption is higher than any other technology category. — [Sage blog](https://www.sage.com/en-us/blog/construction-hiring-and-business-outlook/)
- BuiltWorlds 2025 benchmarking (mostly GCs): ~90% use an estimating solution, 64% on every project. 76% use reality capture with some regularity. 48% use site logistics/management tools on most or every project. 36% use workforce management extensively, up only 3 points since 2024. — [BuiltWorlds field solutions](https://builtworlds.com/news/5-takeaways-2025-field-solutions-benchmarking-report/); [BuiltWorlds preconstruction](https://builtworlds.com/news/one-variety-preconstruction-tech-seeing-widespread-usage/)
- HIRI (home improvement): ~54% of contractors planned to adopt new technology in 2025. Project management software is used by 56% of "high-tech" contractors vs 14% of "low-tech" contractors. — [HIRI](https://www.hiri.org/blog/contractor-tech-adoption-trends)
- Deloitte/Autodesk State of Digital Adoption in Construction 2025 (Asia Pacific): the average business has adopted 6.2 of 16 tracked technologies, up from 5.3 (+20%). — [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/au/en/services/economics/analysis/state-digital-adoption-construction-industry.html)
- **ServiceTitan 2025 AI in the Trades Report** (Thrive Analytics, 1,000+ residential and commercial contractors, fieldwork Oct 23–Nov 12, 2025, published Dec 17, 2025):
  - Adoption and sentiment: 46% are using or experimenting with AI. 72% think AI is relevant to their business. 66% expect it to transform the industry within 1–3 years.
  - How they adopt it: 59% use AI built into software they already have, vs 42% general-purpose tools and 8% custom systems.
  - Use cases by function: administration 59%, marketing/sales 51%, customer service/communication 39%, field operations 39%.
  - Barriers: lack of training/skilled staff and integration challenges tied at 44%, difficulty understanding the tools 38%, unclear ROI 37%.
  - ServiceTitan says the sample is not representative of the whole industry. — [RoofersCoffeeShop](https://www.rooferscoffeeshop.com/post/majority-of-contractors-are-already-seeing-increased-efficiency-from-ai-adoption-servicetitan-industry-report-reveals)
- **Housecall Pro "AI Advantage" report** (248 US home-service pros, May–Jun 2026; 69% owners; 2025 comparison n=420):
  - Adoption: 48% actively use AI (electrical 54%, plumbing 53%). 52% of non-users are unfamiliar with trade AI tools.
  - Top uses among AI users: customer communication and follow-up 52%, estimates/quoting/pricing 51%, business planning/reporting 43%.
  - Results: 40% respond faster and lose fewer leads. More than 1 in 4 save 6+ admin hours a week. 38% are "less buried in paperwork". 68% say AI contributed to revenue growth.
  - Homeowners (2025 HCP survey): 53% are comfortable with AI handling the initial inquiry.
  - Vendor-run survey. — [Housecall Pro, AI in the trades](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/ai-in-the-trades/); [report PDF](https://www.housecallpro.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/062026-HCP-The-AI-Advantage-report.pdf)
- QuickBooks is cited as holding ~62% of the accounting software market. **[weak source: vendor blog quoting ElectroIQ 2025]** — [Knowify](https://knowify.com/resources/best-trade-contractor-software/)
- Vendor buyer guidance lists as non-negotiables: scheduling/dispatch, a "real" mobile app, fast invoicing and payments, customer/asset history, and QuickBooks sync. This is vendor opinion, not survey data. — [Knowify](https://knowify.com/resources/best-trade-contractor-software/)

### Inferences
- Lead capture and response speed is the clearest stated pain. The top AI uses (customer communication 52%, estimating 51%) and the "lose fewer leads" outcome point to missed calls and slow quotes as the main revenue leak for small shops.
- The "59% use AI built into software they already have" figure supports ContractorDocs' embedded AI assistant. Integration and training are the top barriers, so AI that works inside the documents with no setup is the right shape.
- "Adoption is high, use is shallow" (ACCA 56%) suggests simplicity and onboarding count as features for small teams.

### Gaps
- No neutral 2025–2026 survey ranking features (payments vs scheduling vs QuickBooks vs photos) specifically for solo or small contractors was found.
- Capterra/Software Advice 2025–2026 buyer-survey figures (e.g. "top reasons for purchase") were not retrieved.
- The JBKnowledge ConTech Report has not been confirmed as published for 2025–2026 and was not found.
- Jobber's own "state of the industry" stats were not retrieved.

## 3. Major 2025–2026 trends

### Takeaway
Five trends lead in 2025–2026:
1. **Embedded payments**, extended with tap-to-pay, progress invoicing, ACH and bill pay.
2. **Embedded consumer financing**, mainly Wisetack inside Jobber and Housecall Pro.
3. **AI receptionists / AI CSRs** that answer calls and texts. This is the most-marketed AI feature among SMB FSM vendors.
4. **AI "admin" features**: bill/receipt capture, AI client updates, AI estimating and takeoff.
5. **Agentic AI acquisitions** by Procore and Buildertrend.

Compliance (lien waivers) is a growing payments add-on at the GC end. Offline mode and multilingual crews were not covered by any good source found.

### Inferences
- For a small-contractor product, the trends with the most revenue impact are (a) getting paid online, by card or ACH, including deposits and progress payments, and (b) never missing a lead (AI answering, instant text replies, fast quotes). Both appear in the vendor launches and the AI survey stats.
- Lien waivers, COIs and joint checks are mainly a GC/subcontractor (commercial and new-build) need. Small residential/service contractors mostly need simple lien waiver and warranty documents, which fit ContractorDocs' document-template strength.
- AI receptionists are now priced as cheap add-ons (Jobber $29/mo). A $5–$10 product probably can't include voice AI, but could offer AI text auto-replies or a lead form.

- **Voice-note-to-report**: no reliable quantitative source found (Buildertrend's AI Client Updates is the closest).
- **Wisetack volumes, and financing attach rates** inside Jobber or Housecall Pro: not disclosed.

## 4. How vendors price for small teams vs large companies; where the solo/small gap is

### Takeaway
Small-team pricing comes in three shapes:
- **Low-cost single-user tiers that lack key features.** Jobber Core is $29/mo annual but has no QuickBooks sync, automated follow-ups, texting or job costing.
- **Mid tiers with seat bundles at $99–$299/mo.** Getting QuickBooks sync or texting costs $99–$149/mo on Jobber and $149/mo on Housecall Pro.
- **Quote-only enterprise pricing with large onboarding fees.** ServiceTitan is user-reported at $245–$500 per technician per month plus $5K–$50K implementation and a 12-month minimum.

The underserved gap is a solo or 2–5 person contractor who wants payments, QuickBooks sync, follow-ups and professional documents for under ~$30/mo, without per-seat creep or add-on stacking. Only Joist and Invoice2go sit near ContractorDocs' $5–$10 price point, and both are narrower (estimate/invoice only).

## 5. Market-wide must-have features ContractorDocs appears to lack

### Takeaway
ContractorDocs already covers documents, the client portal with e-signature, price book, inventory, profit tracking and AI drafting well. Compared with what the leaders put on entry and mid tiers and what surveys emphasize, the most important likely gaps are:
1. **Integrated online payments** (card/ACH) on invoices, including deposits and progress payments.
2. **QuickBooks (accounting) sync.**
3. **Two-way SMS and automated reminders/follow-ups.**
4. **Consumer financing.**
5. **Lead capture and response**: online booking/request form, AI auto-reply or receptionist.
6. **Scheduling/dispatch** with crew assignment, and a mobile/field experience.
7. **Recurring jobs / maintenance agreements.**
8. **Job photo documentation.**
9. **Compliance documents** such as lien waivers.

### Inferences (prioritized gap list for ContractorDocs, a $0–$10 small-contractor tool)

**Tier 1, highest impact (affects getting paid and winning work)**
1. **Online payments on invoices and the portal** (Stripe-style card plus ACH, deposits on estimate approval, progress/partial payments). ContractorDocs lists e-sign approvals and Gmail sending but no payment collection. Every SMB leader includes it, and it is the market's main monetization lever.
2. **QuickBooks Online (and/or Xero) sync** for invoices, payments and expenses. Competitors gate this behind $99–$149/mo tiers, so offering it at $5–$10 would be a strong differentiator.
3. **Automated reminders/follow-ups and SMS**: unpaid-invoice reminders, estimate follow-ups, appointment reminders. Gmail-only sending misses the text-first channel trades customers use.

**Tier 2, strong differentiators**

4. **Lead capture**: a public request/booking form, plus an AI auto-reply to texts or emails as a cheap version of AI receptionists.
5. **Consumer financing link** on estimates (Wisetack-type partner integration). This costs little to build if done through a partner.
6. **Recurring jobs / maintenance agreements** with auto-generated work orders and invoices.
7. **Job photos** attached to jobs, before/after, inserted into daily reports and portal. Worth checking whether existing documents already support photo galleries.
8. **Scheduling/dispatch** beyond a calendar: assign crew, notify the tech. Also check whether a true mobile/PWA field experience with offline capture exists.

**Tier 3, document-type extensions that fit ContractorDocs' core strength**

9. **New document types**: lien waivers (conditional/unconditional, progress/final), certificate of insurance tracking, permit log, punch list, inspection/safety checklist, progress billing (AIA-style G702/G703 for subs).
10. **AI receipt capture → expense log**, and **voice-note → daily report**. The repo's recent "Voice button" commit may partly cover this, so verify.

**Lower priority for this segment**: digital takeoff, BIM, RFIs/submittals (GC/commercial needs), GPS fleet tracking.

- No survey quantifies how much small contractors value lien waivers, COIs, offline mode or multilingual UI. Their ranking above is an inference.
